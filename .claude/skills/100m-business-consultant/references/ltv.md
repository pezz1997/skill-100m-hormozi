## Core Concept

**Lifetime Value (LTV)** is the gross profit collected over the lifespan of a customer. The fundamental principle is: **the business owner who makes a customer more valuable to his business than to competition wins**.

Since the owner can outspend competitors to acquire customers, they can outbid competitors in every auction-based advertising channel. The Crazy 8 framework provides 8 specific, systematic ways to increase LTV.

Key insight: "Rich people know how to count their money" - Money math is essential to understanding and improving customer value.

---

## The Crazy 8 Framework

These are the eight fundamental ways to increase lifetime value:

### 1. Increase Prices
- Raise the price of what you already sell
- Most direct lever to increase per-transaction profit
- Drives margin improvement

### 2. Decrease Costs
- Reduce the cost to deliver your product/service
- Direct impact on gross profit per customer
- Frees up resources for other improvements

### 3. Increase # of Purchases (Frequency)
- Get customers to buy more often
- Multiplies the number of transactions per customer
- Works for both transactional and recurring models

### 4. Cross-Sell Something Different
- Sell complementary products/services
- Solves customer's next problem (not tangential)
- Increases LTV without changing core offering

### 5. Sell More (Increase Quantity) - Upsell
- Get customers to buy larger quantities per transaction
- Increases revenue per transaction
- Works best when customer needs are greater than initially purchased

### 6. Sell Better (Increase Quality) - Upsell Premium
- Sell premium version of the same product/service
- Customers pay more for better quality
- Can be 20%+ of revenue when implemented correctly

### 7. Downsell Fewer (Lower Quantity)
- Offer smaller quantities to those who can't afford full amount
- Principle: "If it means this or nothing, this beats nothing!"
- Increases per-visitor revenue

### 8. Downsell Lower Quality
- Offer lower-cost version of product/service
- Captures price-sensitive customers who would otherwise buy nothing
- Works only on unqualified prospects (don't cannibalize full offer)

### Framework 2: Churn Calculation Framework (for Recurring Revenue Businesses)

**Definition:** Churn = percentage of customers that leave between time periods

**Formula:** (People who left / Original amount) = Churn %

**Critical Note:** New customers signed up do NOT affect churn calculation. Churn only measures loss from existing customer base.

**Why it matters:**
- Direct impact on LTV in recurring models
- Even small churn reductions create massive LTV improvements
- Example: Reducing 10% churn to 5% churn = 2x LTV per the formula Price/Churn

### Framework 3: Cost Reduction Framework (9 Methods for Decreasing Delivery Costs)

- Increases efficiency without quality loss

- Increase customer-to-employee ratio with minor price decrease
- Customer does more work; you do less
- Requires customer education but maintains quality

#### Method v: Cap Usage (Service Businesses)
- Charge per use after initial cap
- Controls open-ended service delivery
- Works especially well for service businesses
- Example: Include 10 phone calls, then $X per additional call

#### Method vi: Lifetime → Annual Access
- Convert lifetime pricing to annual renewal pricing
- Reduces upfront cost to you
- Creates recurring revenue stream

#### Method vii: In-Person → Remote
- Reduce travel costs by shifting to remote delivery
- Reduces talent sourcing costs (access national/global talent)
- Improves scalability

#### Method viii: Cut Meeting Times
- Delete all recurring meetings
- Identify what MUST be added back
- Halve the duration of remaining meetings
- Typical result: 30-50% reduction in management overhead

#### Method ix: Buy in Bulk & Prepay
- Lock in 10-20% discounts from vendors with prepay commitment
- Guaranteed ROI since discount is immediate
- Improves cash flow predictability

## Formulas

### Formula 1: Lifetime Gross Profit (LTGP) - Transactional Businesses

```
LTGP = Gross Profit × Average # of Transactions Per Customer
```

**Example:**
- Gross Profit per transaction: $80
- Average # of transactions per customer: 4
- LTGP = $80 × 4 = $360 LTGP

**What this tells you:**
- Total profit you expect from average customer over their lifetime
- Baseline to improve with Crazy 8 levers
- If you increase avg transactions to 6: $80 × 6 = $480 (33% improvement)

---

### Formula 2: Lifetime Gross Profit - Recurring Revenue Businesses

```
LTGP = Gross Profit / Churn %
```

**Example:**
- Gross Profit per month: $2,400
- Monthly churn: 5% (0.05)
- LTGP = $2,400 / 0.05 = $48,000 LTGP

**What this tells you:**
- Total profit expected over customer lifetime in recurring model
- Massive sensitivity to churn (small improvements = huge LTV gains)
- Example impact: Reducing churn to 2.5% = $2,400 / 0.025 = $96,000 (2x LTV)

**Why this formula works:**
- Churn creates natural "lifespan" for customer
- If 5% leave each month, average customer lasts 20 months ($2,400 × 20 = $48,000)

---

### Formula 3: Gross Profit Calculation - Product

```
Gross Profit = Price - Cost to Manufacture and Ship
```

**Example:**
- Price: $100
- Cost to manufacture: $15
- Cost to ship: $5
- Gross Profit = $100 - $15 - $5 = $80

**What this tells you:**
- True profit available after manufacturing and logistics costs
- Used as input for LTGP calculations
- Key metric to track as you scale

---

### Formula 4: Gross Profit Calculation - Service (Per Customer)

```
Revenue per Client × Gross Profit % = Gross Profit per Customer
```

**Example:**
- Revenue per client: $3,000/month
- Gross profit percentage: 80%
- Gross Profit per Customer = $3,000 × 0.80 = $2,400/month

**Breaking down Gross Profit %:**
- Gross Profit % = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue
- Example: ($3,000 - $600 COGS) / $3,000 = 80%
- COGS includes: labor, delivery costs, materials, but NOT marketing/overhead

**Alternative calculation (if you know cost per customer):**
- (Revenue per client per period × Clients per rep) - (Cost per rep) = GP per customer
- Example: ($3,000 × 10 clients) - ($20,000 rep cost) = $10,000 GP for that rep

---

### Formula 5: Churn Formula

```
Churn = (People who left from original group / Original group size)
```

**Example:**
- Started month with: 100 customers
- Customers who left during month: 5
- Churn = 5 / 100 = 5% churn

**Critical note:** This is simple monthly churn. To annualize:
- Annual Churn = 1 - (1 - Monthly Churn)^12
- Example: 5% monthly = 1 - (0.95)^12 = 46% annual churn
- This is why small monthly churn reduction matters enormously

---

### Formula 6: Cross-Sell LTV Impact

```
New LTV = Old LTV + (Cross-sell conversion rate × Cross-sell gross profit)
```

**Example:**
- Old LTV: $100 (baseline)
- Cross-sell conversion rate: 20% of customers
- Cross-sell gross profit: $100
- New LTV = $100 + (0.20 × $100) = $100 + $20 = $120 new LTV

**Multiple cross-sells example:**
- Baseline LTV: $100
- Cross-sell 1: 20% × $100 = $20 addition
- Cross-sell 2: 30% × $150 = $45 addition
- Total New LTV = $100 + $20 + $45 = $165 new LTV (65% improvement)

## Step-by-Step Processes

### Process 1: How to Calculate LTV (3 Steps)

This is the foundational process you must complete before implementing the Crazy 8.

#### Step One: Figure Out Gross Profit

**For Products:**
```
Price - COGS = Gross Profit
```
- Document exact cost to manufacture
- Document exact cost to ship
- Calculate per-unit profit
- Action: Document gross profit and gross profit % for everything you sell

**For Services:**
```
(Revenue per client per period × Clients per rep) - (Cost per rep) = GP per customer
```
- Figure out how many clients one rep handles
- Calculate total revenue from that rep's clients
- Subtract rep salary/cost
- Divide by number of clients for GP per customer
- Action: Document gross profit and gross profit % for everything you sell

**Example calculations:**
- Product: $150 price - $30 COGS - $10 shipping = $110 gross profit per unit
- Service: ($5,000/month × 10 clients) - ($25,000 rep cost) = $25,000 GP / 10 = $2,500 GP per customer

#### Step Two: Figure Out Average Customer Transactions or Churn

**For Transactional Business:**
- Export lifetime customer data from your system
- For each customer, count total number of purchases
- Sort by # of transactions
- Calculate average (mean) of the column
- Action: Determine if you have transaction data in your system or use napkin math

**For Recurring Revenue Business:**
- Calculate churn = (People who left / Original customers)
- Example: Started year with 100 customers, 20 left = 20% annual churn
- If this is monthly, calculate: 100 customers, 4 left = 4% monthly churn
- Action: Determine if you have churn data in your CRM or use napkin math
- If you don't have data: estimate based on what you see (if you know most stay 12 months, churn ≈ 8%)

**Example calculations:**
- Transactional: 100 customers, total transactions = 400 total, avg = 4 per customer
- Recurring: Started with 100, 5 left per month on average = 5% monthly churn

#### Step Three: Multiply or Divide

**For Transactional:**
```
Gross profit × avg # transactions = LTGP
```
- Example: $110 × 4 = $440 LTGP

**For Recurring:**
```
Gross profit / Churn % = LTGP
```
- Example: $2,500 / 0.05 = $50,000 LTGP

**Action:** Run the numbers to establish your baseline LTGP. This is the metric you'll improve using the Crazy 8.

**Quick Check:**
- Write down your LTGP number
- This is your starting point
- All Crazy 8 improvements compound on this baseline
- If you 3x your LTGP, you can outspend competitors 3x on customer acquisition

### Process 2: Pricing Test Process (From #1 Increase Prices)

This process tests price increases to find your price ceiling profitably.

#### The Pricing Sweet Spot Concept

- Price that gets most people to buy with highest gross profit maximizes profit
- Remember: Goal is most money, not most sales
- Don't focus on volume; focus on profit margin × volume

#### Implementation Steps

**Step 1: Start Low, Then Go Up**
- Ensure people want product
- Test operations with low price
- Build confidence in market demand

**Step 2: Nudge Price Up by 20% Every ~10 Sales**
- Use 20% increments (aggressive enough to matter, conservative enough to test)
- Track conversion rate at each price point
- Make sure you have statistical significance (10+ sales per price point minimum)

**Step 3: Track Conversion Rate with New Price**
- Monitor how many people buy at each price tier
- Example: At $100, 10% conversion. At $120, 8% conversion. At $144, 4% conversion.
- Calculate break-even point for each price test

**Step 4: Continue Until You See Dramatic Drop in Sales**
- Keep raising price until conversion falls below break-even
- Example: If 10% conversion at $100 = $10 per visitor, at $150 you need 6.67% conversion to break even
- Stop when you hit dramatic dropoff

**Step 5: Go Back to Sweet Spot**
- Identify the price that generated maximum profit (not maximum sales)
- Example: $120 at 8% conversion might generate more profit than $100 at 10% conversion
- ($120 × 0.08 = $9.60 per visitor vs $100 × 0.10 = $10, so $100 wins this example)

**Step 6: Test Again Quarterly**
- Research shows frequent pricing tests = higher profitability
- Plan price tests into your calendar (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4)
- Each quarter's test builds on previous quarter's learnings

#### Break-Even Conversion Rate Calculation

```
Old price × Old conversion rate = New price × New conversion rate (break-even)
```

**Example:**
- Old price: $100
- Old conversion rate: 10%
- Value per visitor: $100 × 0.10 = $10

**Testing new price of $120:**
- Break-even conversion = $10 / $120 = 8.33%
- If you maintain 10% conversion at $120, you profit
- If you drop below 8.33%, you lose money vs old price

**Decision rule:**
- If new conversion > break-even point: You have a winner
- Keep the new price and plan next test for next quarter
- If new conversion < break-even point: Price was too high
- Revert to old price and test a smaller increment next time

### Process 3: Cross-Sell Implementation

This process systematically adds complementary products to increase LTV.

**Step 1: Identify Complementary Product/Service**
- Think about customer's next problem after buying from you
- Look at what other companies sell to your customers
- Survey customers: "What else do you wish we sold?"
- Don't be tangential; it must solve their NEXT problem

**Step 2: Ensure It Solves Customer's Next Problem**
- Not tangential or nice-to-have
- It must be something customer needs (not wants)
- Example: If you sell fitness coaching, next problem is nutrition (not random gift)

**Step 3: Calculate Impact**
- Conversion rate × Gross profit of cross-sell = LTV addition
- If 20% of customers want it, and it's $100 profit, that's $20 addition per customer LTV
- Use this to justify investment in developing/adding cross-sell

**Step 4: Implement Seamlessly**
- Don't break existing operations
- Integrate into current customer journey
- Automate presentation where possible
- Test with small segment first

**Step 5: Deploy Immediately After Initial Purchase**
- Timing: Right after customer buys and is happy
- Email: Send within 24-48 hours of purchase
- Message: "Since you bought [main product], you might also benefit from [cross-sell]"
- Offer: Consider special pricing for immediate add-on

---

### Process 4: Increase Purchase Frequency Via Recurring

This process converts one-time purchasers to recurring customers.

**Step 1: Create Recurring Version of Main Offer**
- Make weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annual option available
- Can be same product on recurring basis
- Or consumable that needs replenishment
- Example: One-time coaching package → monthly coaching subscription

**Step 2: Offer to Existing Customer Base**
- Email existing customers first (lowest acquisition cost)
- Offer as upgrade opportunity
- Offer special recurring pricing for early adopters

**Step 3: Target Conversion: Even 20% Adoption 3x's LTV**
- Don't need high conversion rate for massive impact
- Example: If $100 LTV, 20% of customers adopting recurring → $300 LTV overall (if they stay)
- Focus on value proposition, not volume

**Step 4: If Adding Accountability or Value Service, Even 10% Might Adopt for 5+ Months**
- Monthly accountability calls
- Community access
- Progress tracking
- These create stickiness beyond price

**Example scenario:**
- Current: 100 customers, $100 LTV, $10,000 total LTV
- Add recurring: 20% adoption (20 customers) at $150/month
- New: 20 customers × 6 month average tenure = $18,000
- Plus: 80 customers × $100 = $8,000
- Total: $26,000 (2.6x increase from one lever)

## Actionable Tactics

### 1. Pricing Tactics

**Test Prices Every Quarter**
- Higher frequency = higher profitability per Profitwell research
- Build quarterly price testing into your business calendar
- Plan cadence: Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 tests

**Use 20% Increments When Testing**
- Conservative enough to feel safe
- Aggressive enough to matter
- Creates measurable difference in performance

**Never Stop at First Sweet Spot**
- First sweet spot is not your price ceiling
- Continue testing until you find true ceiling
- Each quarter can yield higher sweet spot

**Add Bonuses for Early Buyers If Price Test Goes Too High**
- If 30% price increase causes 40% conversion drop, backtrack
- Rather than drop price, add bonuses at high price
- Justifies premium price without cutting it
- "Early buyers get $500 in bonus training"

---

### 2. Cost Reduction Tactics

**Audit Your Vendor Contracts**
- Most vendors build in margin for haggling
- Lock in 10-20% bulk discounts with prepay commitment
- Guaranteed ROI since savings are immediate
- Annual opportunity: Review all contracts

**Delete All Recurring Meetings Quarterly**
- Only add back essential meetings
- Halve the duration of remaining meetings
- Result: 30-50% reduction in management overhead
- Frees time for strategic work, not firefighting

**Move From In-Person to Remote**
- Reduces travel costs
- Expands talent sourcing (national/global access)
- Improves cost structure without quality loss
- Easier to scale

**Create Service Templates and Use Automation**
- Document your process for delivering service
- Create templates for common deliverables
- Automate manual tasks where possible
- Increases employee-to-customer ratio

---

### 3. Purchase Frequency Tactics

**Run Quarterly Promotions to Former Customers**
- Reactivate past buyers at lower cost than new acquisition
- Offer something new, not same thing
- Example: Q1 = pricing sale, Q2 = new product, Q3 = bundle, Q4 = premium tier

**Provide 75% Value Content, 25% Asks**
- Keep list warm by sending value
- Three value emails, then one ask
- Build goodwill so asks convert better

**Build Community Features to Increase Engagement and Retention**
- Private community for customers
- Peer support reduces support burden
- Network effects create lock-in
- Community members churn less

**Add Monthly Accountability Calls**
- Scheduled once per month with customers
- Check on progress, troubleshoot issues
- Creates obligation to keep using service
- Increases stickiness and retention

### 4. Quality Upsell Tactics

**Present Premium Version FIRST on Sales Calls**
- Then downsell standard offer
- Anchors customer perception of value
- Standard offer seems affordable in comparison
- Premium creates context

**In Follow-Up Meetings, Upgrade Existing Customers to Higher Tier**
- After customer gets results on standard tier
- Present premium tier with additional benefits
- Timing: When they're happy and see value
- Common 20%+ lifts from quality upsells

**Common 20%+ Lifts From Quality Upsells When Implemented Correctly**
- This is conservative estimate; many see 30%+
- Requires clear differentiation between tiers
- Premium must offer real value, not fake upgrades

**Focus on Premium Service Dimensions**
- Faster response (most valuable)
- Better people (senior vs junior)
- More access (time, availability)
- These justify 3-5x price premiums

---

### 5. Downsell Tactics

**Only Offer to Prospects Who Don't Qualify for Main Offer**
- Not every prospect; specifically the unqualified
- Example: Budget constraint, technical limitation
- Never position as "cheaper option" - position as "right-sized"

**Never Let Qualified Prospects Opt Down**
- This cannibalizes your premium offer
- Qualified means: has budget, needs full solution, fits your profile
- Unqualified means: doesn't have budget or isn't ready

**Can Generate 20%+ More Revenue Per Visitor**
- Even though individual transaction is smaller
- Because downsell captures customer you'd otherwise lose
- Example: 10% conversion on main offer, 20% conversion on downsell among remaining 90%

**Low Operational Drag Since You Already Deliver the Product**
- You have process, training, templates
- Downsell uses same systems
- Only variable cost difference (which is small)

---

### 6. General Tactics

**Survey Customers Before Launching New Offers**
- Ask: "What do you want to buy from us next?"
- Not: "What do you think we should sell?"
- Ask customers, not internal team

**Run Through All 8 Frameworks for Any Business**
- Pick 2-3 Crazy 8s to focus on
- Avoid attempting all 8 at once (overwhelm)
- Best sequence: Price first, then frequency, then upsells

**Follow Up Every Quarter With Some Engagement/Promotion**
- Keeps business top of mind
- Prevents churn from inactivity
- Opportunity to test new offers

## Key Metrics & Benchmarks

### Customer Value Benchmarks

**1 out of 9 Americans is a millionaire**
- Can afford premium pricing
- Identifies addressable market for quality upsells
- 1/9 = 11% of population has wealth to upgrade

**10x the 10% Rule: Get 10% of customers to pay 10x price = 2x revenue**
- If 10% of customers pay 10x normal price
- This generates 2x total revenue
- Example: 100 customers × $100 = $10K. 10 customers × $1,000 = $10K extra = $20K total
- Massively profitable because high-ticket customers have 90%+ margins

### Profit Impact Benchmarks

**Pricing increases have MORE impact on profit than any other single variable**
- 6x more impact than acquisition improvements
- 2x more impact than retention improvements
- Highest ROI activity in business

**10% profit business + 20% price increase with same sales = 3x business (not 20% growth)**
- Example: $10,000 revenue, $8,000 costs = $2,000 profit (20% margin)
- Raise price 20%: $12,000 revenue, $8,000 costs = $4,000 profit (33% margin)
- 2x profit = 3x business growth from single lever
- This is non-linear compounding

**Example: $100 LTV × 4 transactions = $360 LTGP (baseline to improve)**
- This is starting point
- Using Crazy 8, realistic targets:
  - Price +20%: $432 LTGP

**Cost reduction only goes to zero, but pricing and frequency go infinitely high**
- Cost management: Can only cut to zero
- Pricing: Can increase indefinitely
- Frequency: Can compound exponentially
- Strategy: Price and frequency have more upside than cost cutting

**Even modest churn reduction has massive LTV impact: 10%→5% churn = 2x LTV**
- Using LTGP = Profit / Churn formula
- Cut churn from 10% to 5% = double LTV
- Example: $2,400 / 0.10 = $24,000. $2,400 / 0.05 = $48,000
- Largest leverage in recurring business model
