## Core Concept

Raising prices is the fastest way to increase profitability without acquiring new customers. Most business owners operate in a "vicious price cycle" where they compete on price and destroy their business. Smart operators use a "virtuous price cycle" where higher prices fund better service, creating a sustainable competitive advantage.

## Key Frameworks

### Framework 1: THE VICIOUS PRICE CYCLE (What to Avoid)

When you DECREASE prices:
- Clients' emotional investment DECREASES
- Perceived value of service DECREASES
- Results DECREASE due to decreased investment and perceived value
- Clients INCREASE their demandingness
- Clients get LESS service (less budget to serve them)

**Business impact:**
- DECREASES profit per customer
- DECREASES perceived self-value
- DECREASES ability to create results for customers
- DECREASES level of service per customer
- DECREASES conviction in sales process
- DECREASES gratitude for customers (feel underappreciated)

### Framework 2: THE VIRTUOUS PRICE CYCLE (What to Do)

When you INCREASE prices:
- Clients' emotional investment INCREASES
- Perceived value of service INCREASES
- Results INCREASE due to increased investment and perceived value
- Clients DECREASE their demandingness
- Clients get MORE service (more budget to serve them)

**Business impact:**
- INCREASES profit per customer
- INCREASES perceived self-value
- INCREASES ability to create results for customers
- INCREASES level of service per customer
- INCREASES conviction in sales process (can guarantee delivery)

### Framework 3: RAISE - THE 5-SECTION PRICE RAISE LETTER FRAMEWORK

The RAISE framework provides the structure for your price increase communication:

**R - Remind:** Remind customers of value already provided
- Highlight value already delivered (number of calls, features used, revenue generated, profit increases)
- Personalize as much as possible with customer data
- Make it about them, not you

**A - Address:** Address the price change directly (rip off bandaid)
- Single sentence stating price increase is needed to continue investing
- Example: "For us to continue to invest in making THE PRODUCT for you, we need to increase our prices"
- Rip off bandaid, don't waste time

**I - Invest:** Frame increase in context of future investments they'll benefit from
- Frame as investments that benefit them specifically
- Categories: hiring better people, training, equipment, technology/software, facility upgrades, higher service level
- Pair each investment with specific benefit (faster/easier/more/better/risk-free)
- Only mention investments you're already planning to make
- Don't add new expenses to justify price raise

**S - Soften:** Soften blow with loyalty reward (expiring discount)
- Announce new price for new customers
- Offer existing customers discount for 3-6 months as loyalty reward
- Frame as "giving them credit" rather than raising price
- After discount period ends, customers move to full new price
- Option: Stair-step discount (e.g., $200/mo off 4 months, then $100/mo off, then $50/mo off)

**E - Explain:** Explain away concerns with PS statement offering help if needed
- Include strong PS statement addressing their concerns
- B2C version: "If this materially affects your ability to buy groceries, let me know and we'll work something out"
- B2B version: "If this materially impacts your business, let me know and we'll work something out"
- Be ready to have 1-on-1 conversations with customers
- Have optionality to extend discount or offer smaller incremental price jumps

### Framework 4: PRICING OPTIMIZATION FRAMEWORK

Variables that impact pricing decisions:
- Price point ($)
- Clicks to website
- Conversion rate (%)
- Number of sales
- Churn rate (%)
- Lifetime value (LTV)
- Total return ($)

**Key principle:** When you double price, you typically lose 20% conversion but gain 60% revenue increase overall

### Framework 5: CUSTOMER RESPONSE TYPOLOGY

**Type 1: People who see value**
- Will accept price increase because they get sufficient value

**Type 2: People actually affected**
- Will write sob stories, giving you optionality to extend discount

**Type 3: People already leaving**
- Will cancel sooner, just "pulling forward" churn by 1 month

## Formulas

### Formula 1: Basic Pricing Test Formula

For every 100 clicks:
```
Conversions = Clicks × Conversion Rate
LTV = Price × (1 / Churn Rate) × Average Months Retained
Total Return = Conversions × LTV
```

**Example:**
- $10 price, 5% conversion rate
- 100 clicks = 5 sales
- $100 LTV
- $500 total return

**Calculation breakdown:**
- If churn rate is 1% per month and customer retained 10 months
- LTV = $10 × (1 / 0.01) × 10 = $10,000
- Total return = 5 × $10,000 = $50,000

### Formula 2: Price Increase Impact

```
Revenue Change % = (New Price × New Conversion Rate) - (Old Price × Old Conversion Rate) / (Old Price × Old Conversion Rate)
```

**Example: When doubling price (100% increase) with 20% conversion drop**
- Old: $10 × 5% = $0.50 per click
- New: $20 × 4% = $0.80 per click
- Change: ($0.80 - $0.50) / $0.50 = 60% revenue increase

**Key principle:** You can afford significant conversion rate drops and still increase revenue

### Formula 3: Profit Per Customer Formula

```
Profit Per Customer = (Price - Cost of Delivery) × Average Months Retained / Number of Customers
```

**Example:**
- Price: $100/month
- Cost of delivery: $30/month
- Profit margin per month: $70
- Average months retained: 12
- Profit per customer: $70 × 12 = $840

## Step-by-Step Processes

### Process 1: How to Pick Your Price

Follow these 8 steps to optimize your price point:

**Step 1:** Set baseline pricing (e.g., $10/month)

**Step 2:** Calculate baseline metrics:
- Clicks to website
- Conversion rate percentage
- Number of sales
- Churn rate
- Lifetime value (LTV)
- Total return

**Step 3:** Test doubled price (e.g., $20/month)

**Step 4:** Measure results at new price:
- New conversion rate
- New churn rate
- New LTV

**Step 5:** Calculate total return at new price

**Step 6:** If revenue increased, proceed to step 7. If decreased, revert and try smaller increment.

**Step 7:** Test incremental price points ($39-$59) to find optimal price

**Step 8:** Base decision on which price point generates highest total return

### Process 2: How to Execute a Price Raise (7-Step Implementation)

**Step 1:** Decide on price increase amount
- Determine percentage increase (10%, 25%, 50%, 100%, etc.)
- Consider market tolerance
- Calculate expected revenue impact

**Step 2:** TEST WITH NEW CUSTOMERS FIRST
- Verify that new customers will buy at higher price without massive churn
- This gives you confidence for existing customer pitch
- Collect data showing new customers accepting price

**Step 3:** Segment out old customers who came before price raise
- Separate cohorts: customers before raise vs. after
- Apply different pricing/communications to each group
- Only offer discounts/loyalty rewards to existing customers

**Step 4:** Write 1-5 bullets of current value provided to each customer
- Personalize heavily with customer-specific data
- Include metrics: calls held, features used, revenue/profit generated
- Make it specific to their journey, not generic

**Step 5:** Write 3 bullets of biggest investments you'll make with increased profit
- Only mention investments you're already doing
- Categories: hiring, training, equipment, technology, facility, service level
- Pair each with specific customer benefit

**Step 6:** Explain how investments benefit them
- "Faster service through better hiring"
- "More reliable platform through tech investment"
- "Better results through trained team"
- Frame each investment as customer benefit, not company cost

**Step 7:** Give expiring discount as loyalty reward
- 3-6 month discount period
- Optional: Stair-step discount declining over time
- Example: "You keep your current price for 3 months as our thank you"

### Process 3: Crafting the Price Raise Letter

**Step 1 - R (Remind):**
- Highlight value already delivered:
  - Number of calls/sessions held
  - Features used or activated
  - Revenue generated for them
  - Profit increases they've achieved
- Personalize as much as possible with customer data
- Make it about them, not you
- Example: "Over the last 8 months, we've completed 24 coaching sessions together, and you've grown your revenue by $120,000"

**Step 2 - A (Address):**
- Single sentence stating price increase is needed
- Example: "For us to continue to invest in making THE PRODUCT for you, we need to increase our prices"
- Rip off bandaid immediately
- Don't waste time with preamble
- Be direct but not apologetic

**Step 3 - I (Invest):**
- Frame as investments that benefit them specifically
- Investment categories:
  - Hiring better people
  - Training and skill development
  - Equipment upgrades
  - Technology/software investments
  - Facility improvements
  - Higher service level
- For each investment, pair with specific benefit:
  - Faster response times
  - Easier implementation
  - More personalization
  - Better results
  - Risk-free guarantees
- Only mention investments you're already planning to make
- Don't add new expenses to justify price raise
- Example structure: "We're investing in [specific people/tech/resources] so you get [specific benefit]"

**Step 4 - S (Soften):**
- Announce new price for new customers moving forward
- Offer existing customers discount for loyalty:
  - 3-6 month discount period is standard
  - Frame as "giving them credit" rather than raising price
  - After discount period ends, customers move to full new price
- Alternative: Stair-step discount structure
  - Example: $200/month off for 4 months, then $100/month off for 2 months, then $50/month off for 1 month
  - Then full new price
- This makes the increase feel more manageable

**Step 5 - E (Explain):**
- Include strong PS statement addressing concerns
- B2C version: "If this materially affects your ability to buy groceries, let me know and we'll work something out"
- B2B version: "If this materially impacts your business, let me know and we'll work something out"
- This shows empathy while maintaining the price increase
- Be ready to have 1-on-1 conversations with customers who respond
- Have optionality to:
  - Extend discount period
  - Offer smaller incremental price jumps
  - Create custom arrangements for special cases

### Process 4: Handling Incoming New Customers (During Price Raise)

**Step 1:** Price new customers at new, higher rate immediately
- Don't grandfather old pricing for anyone
- No exceptions in sales process

**Step 2:** If they mention old price, sales team responds: "That's actually good news"
- Acknowledge they found old information
- Don't apologize for price increase

**Step 3:** Explain three-part narrative:
- **Part 1:** "We keep making value better" (product/service improvement narrative)
- **Part 2:** "96% of customers agreed" (social proof of acceptance)
- **Part 3:** "This is cheapest it'll ever be" (scarcity/future increase hint)

**Step 4:** Complete the sale at new price
- People will complain but will still buy
- Don't discount or offer special pricing
- This proves the new price point works

### Process 5: Post-Price Raise Measurement (Track Churn Pattern)

Track these milestones:

**Month 1:** You'll see INCREASE in churn
- This is expected and normal
- Some price-sensitive customers will leave
- Don't panic - this is the pull-forward of future churn
- This is Type 3 customers (already leaving)

**Month 2:** Churn DECREASES
- Should be lower than normal baseline
- Shows you "pulled forward" churn successfully
- Type 1 customers (see value) stay and remain loyal
- Type 2 customers (affected) either accept discount or leave

**Month 3:** Churn returns to baseline or better
- Should match or beat pre-raise churn rates
- Indicates successful execution
- New higher price is stabilized
- Revenue increase is permanent

**Interpretation:**
- If churn stays elevated beyond month 3 = pricing was too aggressive for market
- If churn returns to baseline by month 2 = pricing was just right
- If churn increases = price increase was too high or implementation was poor

## Actionable Tactics

### Tactic 1: Warren Buffett Pricing Rule

Raise prices at least once per year, minimum.

**Implementation:**
- Buffett raised See's Candies prices 10% annually
- Some years he went up to 17%
- Set price raises as a yearly recurring business decision like Warren does
- Don't wait for "perfect timing" - build it into annual rhythm
- Conservative approach: 10% per year
- Aggressive approach: up to 17% per year

**Benefit:** This keeps you ahead of inflation and continuously improves margins

### Tactic 2: Test-First Approach

Always test new prices on new customers before existing customer base.

**Implementation:**
- Announce new price to new customers only first
- Wait 1-2 weeks for new customer feedback
- Collect data showing new customers accepting price
- Gives you proof the new price works
- New customers saying yes gives confidence for existing customer pitch
- Shows market already agrees with higher value = less pushback

**Benefit:** You have concrete data to share with existing customers: "New customers are accepting this price"

### Tactic 3: Community Communication Strategy

For businesses with member community, use strategic communication approach.

**Implementation:**
- If you have member community, post video explaining change (like Trey's gym did)
- TURN OFF COMMENTS on posts
- Refer all questions to direct conversation
- Enforce through PS statement in letter
- This prevents public complaints/gossip mill
- Keeps conversation personal and manageable

**Benefit:** Controls narrative, prevents negative sentiment from spreading, allows individual customization

### Tactic 4: Volume-Based Pricing Tactic

For customers heavily affected by large raises, do it in increments.

**Implementation:**
- For very large raises: split into 1-3 increments instead of all at once
- Stair-step approach example:
  - Month 0-4: $200/month discount off new price
  - Month 4-6: $100/month discount off new price
  - Month 6-7: $50/month discount off new price
  - Month 7+: Full new price
- Easier for customers to accept gradual increases than one large jump
- Better for cash flow management on customer side

**Benefit:** Increases acceptance, reduces churn, feels less like price shock

### Tactic 5: Price Raise When Launching New Features

Pair price raise within 90 days of product/promotion launch.

**Implementation:**
- Launch new feature or major update
- Within 30-90 days, announce price increase
- Frame as "early adopter pricing" to leverage launch momentum
- Takes advantage of natural attention/excitement
- New feature justifies new price in customers' minds
- Existing customers already see value from new feature

**Benefit:** Rides wave of positive sentiment, justifies price increase naturally

### Tactic 6: Meeting Requirement for Large Price Increases

For 50%+ price increases, meet with customers 1-on-1.

**Implementation:**
- Only required for increases above 50%
- Schedule phone/video call with each customer
- Walk through value provided and future investments
- Answer their specific concerns
- Offer customized arrangements if needed
- Example: Shopify competitor went from $30 to $3000/month (100x increase), lost only 1 of 300 customers through personal 1-on-1 calls

**Benefit:** Dramatically reduces churn on large increases, maintains relationships

### Tactic 7: Segmentation Strategy

Segment old customers from new customers before execution.

**Implementation:**
- Identify exact cutoff date for "existing" vs. "new" customers
- Create two customer groups in your system
- Send customized letters to each:
  - Old customers: include discount period and loyalty offer
  - New customers: new price only, no discount offered
- Track separately in your analytics
- This ensures fairness and clear implementation

**Benefit:** Clean execution, no confusion, no retroactive complaints

### Tactic 8: Signature Requirement

Sign physical letters in ink if possible. If email, sign with your name personally.

**Implementation:**
- Print letters and sign in blue ink (shows it's genuine)
- Mail physical letters for major customers
- For email: hand-sign name at bottom, don't use automated signature
- Include your photo or personal message
- Creates credibility and shows personal commitment

**Benefit:** Increases perceived legitimacy, shows leadership committed, more likely to be read and accepted

### Tactic 9: Real-World Price Raise Case Study - Trey's Gym

Learn from this successful implementation:

**Starting situation:**
- $49/month memberships
- Losing money ($2,000/month negative)
- Unsustainable business model

**Action taken:**
- Tripled prices (likely to ~$150/month)
- Implemented in 1 week
- Used clear communication about investments

**Results:**
- Lost some members (expected)
- Went from -$2,000/month to +$4,000/month profit
- 4x month swing = $6,000 improvement
- Maintained core customer base

**Key learning:**
- "In 12 more weeks, you won't have a gym if we don't raise prices"
- It's do or die sometimes
- Act decisively rather than slowly
- Choose survival over temporary popularity

**Lesson:** If your business model is broken, radical price increase often saves it

## Key Metrics and Benchmarks

### Benchmark 1: Buffett Benchmark

10% annual price increase is the standard.

**Conservative approach:**
- 10% per year
- Keeps pace with inflation
- Builds sustainable margin growth

**Aggressive approach:**
- Up to 17% per year
- See's Candies historical precedent
- Works for premium, quality-based businesses

**Minimum standard:**
- Raise prices at least once annually to match inflation and maintain margins

**Calculation:**
- If inflation is 3% and you raise prices 10%, you're gaining 7% real margin improvement
- Over 10 years, this doubles your margins: (1.10)^10 = 2.59x

### Benchmark 2: Price Sensitivity Thresholds

Expected conversion rate drops at different price increase levels:

**At 100% price increase (2x):**
- Expect ~20% conversion rate drop
- Net result: 60% revenue increase overall
- Usually sustainable for most businesses

**At 1000% price increase (10x):**
- Expect ~60% conversion rate drop
- 40% of original customers remain
- Revenue increases 3x even with 60% loss

**Sweet spot typically:**
- 2-3x price increase range
- Revenue positive with manageable customer loss
- 20-40% conversion rate drop is acceptable range

**Formula for prediction:**
- If doubling price, expect ~20% conversion loss = 60% revenue gain
- Each additional 100% price increase adds ~20% more conversion loss
- For 3x increase: expect ~40% conversion loss = 80% revenue gain

### Benchmark 3: Churn Pattern Benchmarks

Track these patterns post-implementation:

**Month 1 increase in churn:**
- Expected and normal
- Some customers price-sensitive
- Type 3 customers (already leaving) exit sooner
- Don't panic at elevated churn

**Month 2 should show:**
- Churn returning down toward baseline
- Shows you "pulled forward" churn successfully
- Indicates successful implementation
- Churn should be lower than normal for this month

**Month 3 return to baseline:**
- Should match or beat pre-raise churn rates
- Indicates successful execution long-term
- New higher price is stabilized
- Revenue increase is permanent

**Red flags:**
- If churn stays elevated beyond month 3 = pricing was too aggressive
- If churn keeps increasing in months 2-3 = messaging or market fit issue
- If initial churn is 50%+ = pricing was unrealistic for market

### Benchmark 4: Customer Response Expectations

Real-world data on customer acceptance:

**Shopify case study:**
- Increase: $30 to $3000/month (100x price increase)
- Customer loss: Only 1 of 300 customers (0.3% loss rate)
- Success factor: 1-on-1 personal calls
- Lesson: Relationship investment prevents churn

**Trey's gym case study:**
- Increase: $49 to ~$150/month (3x increase)
- Customer loss: Some members left
- Financial impact: -$2,000/month to +$4,000/month ($6,000 swing)
- Lesson: Radical increase can save unsustainable business

**General expectation:**
- You won't lose "all" customers despite fears
- 20-40% lose rate is typical for 2-3x increases
- 60-80% customer retention is common outcome
- Better service/product justifies higher retention

### Benchmark 5: Revenue Impact

Financial impact of different price increase scenarios:

**Doubling price (100% increase) with 20% conversion drop:**
- Old: $10 price × 5% conversion = $0.50 per click
- New: $20 price × 4% conversion = $0.80 per click
- Revenue increase: 60% per click
- Profit increase (at 30% margins): 3x profit

**Tripling price (200% increase) with 40% conversion drop:**
- Old: $10 price × 5% conversion = $0.50 per click
- New: $30 price × 3% conversion = $0.90 per click
- Revenue increase: 80% per click
- Profit increase (at 30% margins): ~5x profit

**10x price increase (900% increase) with 60% conversion drop:**
- Old: $10 price × 5% conversion = $0.50 per click
- New: $100 price × 2% conversion = $2.00 per click
- Revenue increase: 300% per click
- Profit increase (at 30% margins): ~10x profit

**Lesson:** Even large customer losses can result in significant revenue and profit increases
